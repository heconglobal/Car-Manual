# Suspension nominal-data comparison — September 22, 2026

Primary page inspected: Pontiac 1985 MVMA, issued September 1, 1984, printed page 11 / local `1985-86-specifications.pdf` PDF 13. [Original form](https://www.boomtastic.com/files/?serve_file=Service+Manuals%2C+Guides%2C+and+Tips%2FMotor+Vehicle+Specifcations%2F1985-86+Pontiac+Fiero.PDF#page=13).

The page explicitly labels spring rates as 31.5 N/mm base and 36.5 N/mm with WS6 at the front, and 40.0 / 44.0 N/mm at the rear. It gives front jounce/rebound 64/96 mm and rear 62/120 mm, a 23 mm front stabilizer and no rear stabilizer. These are nominal form values; installed spring codes and physical load conditions still require verification.

The front spring size row contains two sets (193 × 87.5; 2744 × 12.2 mm and 212 × 87.5; 2863 × 12.4 mm), but does not individually label those two sets as base/WS6. The row heading defines design height and inside diameter, followed by bar length and diameter. Do not silently turn the second set into a certified WS6 part identity without additional application evidence. The rear size row gives 200 × 166; 2700 × 15.6 mm. The rear wheel-rate entry prints 95.1 N/mm alongside 257.7 lb/in, which are inconsistent; it is excluded from verified data.

The current reconstruction uses a front coil mean radius of 68 mm and wire radius 6.9 mm, and a rear mean radius of 72 mm with wire radius 7.3 mm. These do not match the nominal size row. Correcting them requires coordinating spring seats, shock/strut location, tower height and wheel clearance. Scaling a coil in isolation would create new assembly interference. Winding count, end form, loaded installation height and exact spring application are still unverified; do not infer them from spring-rate arithmetic alone.

This discrepancy is retained under requirements 09.1, 09.2 and 09.4. The existing interaction regression does not certify these spring dimensions or full wheel clearance.
